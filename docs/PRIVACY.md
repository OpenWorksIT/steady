# Privacy and POPIA notes

These are engineering notes to help the practice and its advisers. They are not legal advice.

## Roles

- The **practice** is the responsible party under POPIA. It decides why and how patient information is processed.
- **OpenWorks IT** hosts and maintains Steady for the practice and is an **operator**. Section 21 requires a written contract between them that obliges the operator to keep the information secure and confidential. That contract is the **operator agreement** ([OPERATOR-AGREEMENT.md](OPERATOR-AGREEMENT.md)), which the master clinician signs in the app.
- Check-ins, questionnaire answers and notes are **health information**, which is special personal information (sections 26 to 33). Processing by health professionals under a duty of confidentiality for the patient's care is the usual basis (section 32); the practice should confirm this with its adviser and the HPCSA rules it follows.

## What the system does

| Requirement | How Steady handles it |
|---|---|
| First-launch notice | Everyone sees a plain-language privacy and POPIA notice (`app/lib/content/privacy_notice.dart`) before signing in. The version and time they acknowledged it are stored on their account. |
| Watch data | Optional and off by default. The patient sees exactly what is shared and agrees separately; that consent is stored with version and time, and they can withdraw it in Settings (uploads stop; what was shared stays in the record). The watch consent (`wearable-2`) says shared watch data may be added to their clinical record, where their psychologist decides how it is used in their care. Only daily summaries leave the phone. No alerts are raised from watch data: RADR uses it only to order the psychologist's list, after answers waiting for review ([RADR.md](RADR.md)). |
| Consent and openness | Patients read the practice's consent statement and tick to agree when they join. The version they accepted and when are stored on their account. |
| Operator agreement | The written contract section 21 requires, signed in the app: the master clinician is shown it the first time they open Steady, and again for each new version, and can't go on until they sign (other staff aren't asked). It covers processing only on the practice's instructions, confidentiality, the security measures in this table, OpenWorks' sub-operators, transfers abroad (device tokens for notifications only), notice of a security compromise within 24 hours and OpenWorks' breach plan, help with patients' requests, deletion, and the end of the agreement. It records the practice's details and its **Information Officer**: the master clinician, unless the practice names someone else. Each signature is kept with the exact text and its SHA-256, who signed, when, from which address and app; nobody can change or delete it, and signing again (new details) adds a new one. The master clinician, the practice admin and OpenWorks support open it in Settings, with a PDF; there is no patient information in it. `accounts.sh check` and the fleet view say whether the current version is signed, and `audit-server.sh` fails until it is. |
| Minimality | Name, email, four ratings, optional free text, questionnaire answers. No location or contacts. Technical details: a push token, the app version and kind of phone, the time zone, and the internet address in the access log (all in the consent statement and the privacy notice). |
| Access control | Patients see only their own data. Psychologists see only patients linked to them, plus any patient a colleague shared with them (read only, one patient at a time, and the patient can see who in Settings). The **master clinician** sees their own patients' data and, for everyone, connection status. The **practice admin** sees patients' names and whether each one is set up and connected (agreements accepted, app version, notifications, watch sharing and permission, last check-in and upload dates), never what they shared. **OpenWorks support** sees the practice's staff (names, status, app versions) and the server, and nothing about patients, not even names. Enforced by the server, not by the app. (Outside the app, OpenWorks staff with root on the server can technically reach its data, to keep it running; the operator agreement covers that, and the consent statement says so.) |
| Staff sign-in | Psychologists, the practice admin and OpenWorks support sign in with a password, a code from an authenticator app, **and a device they've linked**. A new phone or browser is linked with a 6-digit code emailed to the staff member from the Steady mailbox (these emails never carry patient information). On phones, the app locks when it opens and after two minutes in the background, and unlocks with the phone's own screen lock. Used codes can't be replayed; eight one-time recovery codes cover a lost authenticator. |
| Confirm it's you | Before a password reset, stopping or restoring an account, adding staff, an export, reading the audit report or removing a device, staff confirm it's them (the phone's screen lock, or an authenticator code on the web). Checked by the server; it lasts five minutes. |
| Lost or stolen phone | A staff member can lock their own account from any device with their email and password; it signs them out everywhere and unlinks every device. The master clinician, the practice admin and OpenWorks support can also stop a staff account. Only OpenWorks support restores one, after checking it's really the person. Staff are emailed when their password is reset, their account is stopped or restored, or a device is linked or removed. |
| Password resets | Done by people at the practice, never by an emailed link. Patients: their psychologist, the master clinician or the practice admin. Psychologists: the master clinician, the practice admin or OpenWorks support. The practice admin: the master clinician or OpenWorks support. The master clinician: OpenWorks support. A reset of a patient by the practice admin or master clinician is finished only with a 6-digit code emailed to the patient (worded without naming the practice), so whoever reset it can't choose the new password and read the patient's data; without email on the server, only the patient's own psychologist can reset a patient. Clearing someone's authenticator (a full reset) is OpenWorks support only. A reset gives a temporary password, signs the person out everywhere and unlinks a staff member's devices; until they choose a new one the account can't read or send anything, so whoever did the reset can't use it to look at the patient's data. Every reset is logged and the psychologist is told. |
| Exports | A psychologist can export their own patients' records (PDF report, Excel workbook, or a text PDF for AI note-taking) for the clinical file or a data request. Each export states the methods used (RADR version, flag rules, Steady build) and whether each flag, RADR's included, was marked as reviewed. Exports are logged. The file is then the practice's responsibility: keep it in the patient file, not in email or a downloads folder. |
| Sorted (practice software) | If the practice connects Sorted, it reads a treating psychologist's own patients' feed server to server with a key kept in each server's settings. Item-level answers are left out. Every read is logged on both sides. |
| Email | Only about the person's own account, never anything they or anyone else shared: to staff, device-link codes and notices; to patients, a reset code and their deletion request and its completion, worded without naming the practice. Sent from the Steady mailbox (steadyapp.co.za) through the server's own mail settings. PocketBase's built-in reset-link, sign-in-code, email-change and "new sign-in location" emails are switched off. |
| No dashboard | PocketBase's built-in admin dashboard is switched off. Account work on the server goes through `accounts.sh`, which leaves no standing superuser behind. |
| Brute-force protection | Passwords of at least 10 characters. Sign-in, password reset and invite codes are rate limited per address. |
| Access logging | Every read of patient data through the API (check-ins, results, watch data and a psychologist's notifications, as lists, single records and live updates; a patient's summary, notes, RADR view, RADR explanations and reviews, and exports; and each patient on the overview, once a day) is written to `auditlog` with who, what and when, before the answer goes out: if the row can't be written, nothing is shown. Rows keep the patient's id after the account is deleted. The master clinician and the practice admin read it in the app (Settings > Audit report), for example for a patient's access request; reading it is logged too. OpenWorks can also print it on the server with `accounts.sh audit`. Shared reads, exports, resets, stops, sign-in codes, device links and the Sorted feed are logged too. |
| Security in transit | HTTPS only (Caddy, automatic certificates, HSTS). |
| Security at rest | Encrypt the server's disk. Backups are encrypted with `age` before they're written. |
| Push notifications | Every notification is titled "Steady" and says "New notification", nothing else: no names, scores or text, not even what kind of thing is new, and no ids. iPhones get them through Apple (APNs), Android phones through Google (Firebase Cloud Messaging, in OpenWorks IT's Firebase project). The server tells its push relay only which devices to notify; the relay sends Apple or Google the device's token and those words. The app loads what's new over its own connection when it's opened. The web app has no notifications. |
| Retention | In-app notifications are deleted after 90 days (configurable). Clinical records (check-ins, results) are kept as part of the patient file; the practice decides the period under its record-keeping obligations. |
| Deleting an account | A patient asks in the app (Settings > Delete my account, with their password). Their psychologist is told in the app and has 30 days: export what belongs in the clinical record (HPCSA Booklet 9: at least six years after the record becomes dormant, until a minor's 21st birthday, for life for a patient who is mentally incompetent), then delete the account on the patient's page. Everything Steady holds about the patient goes; the access log stays. The patient is emailed when they ask and when it's done. |
| Stopping | A psychologist can stop a patient's access; the account can't sign in and waiting activities are cancelled. Records stay for the file. |
| Location | Host in South Africa. Section 72 limits sending personal information abroad. |
| Phones | The session token and the device key are stored in the iOS Keychain / Android Keystore. Android backups of app data are disabled. In a browser, a staff session lives only in that tab (closing it signs out); signing out ends the account's sessions on the server too. |

## For the practice to do

1. Sign the operator agreement with OpenWorks IT: the master clinician does this in the app the first time they open Steady. It names the practice's Information Officer (the master clinician unless the practice names someone else).
2. Read Steady's consent statement (`server/pb_hooks/content/consent.md`), which its patients agree to; its contact line comes from the operator agreement. A practice that needs its own statement has OpenWorks install it with its branding (`consent.md`, [BRAND.md](BRAND.md)). Approve the crisis wording too.
3. Register its Information Officer with the Information Regulator if it hasn't.
4. Decide retention periods.
5. Decide whether PHQ-9 (with item 9) or PHQ-8 is used between sessions, and how out-of-hours results are handled.
6. Keep a breach response plan (section 22 requires notifying the Regulator and patients). OpenWorks' part is in the operator agreement (clauses 9 and 10): notice to the Information Officer within 24 hours, and help with the practice's notifications.

## Watch data and RADR

RADR uses watch data and check-ins to improve care: it shows the psychologist how far a patient is from their own normal and orders their list. Kempie van Rooyen (VR Clinical Psychology) approved it for use in care, and its ethics and patient consent sit with him. If watch data is ever used for research beyond care (for example the design of Amon Dîn), the practice decides whether ethics approval and a research-specific consent are needed in addition to the in-app consent.

## Things to keep in mind

- **The test setup is not for real patient data.** Through a Cloudflare tunnel, Cloudflare decrypts the traffic at its edge (outside South Africa) and the server is a test PC. Move to the South African VPS before real patients are invited; see the security review.

- The free-text box can contain anything, including disclosures of risk. The app says it isn't monitored around the clock; psychologists should agree how often they look.
- Patients' phones are outside the practice's control. Encourage a screen lock.
- Notifications pass through Apple's and Google's servers, outside South Africa. What they carry
  is a device token and the words "New notification" (the consent statement and the privacy
  notice say so). The Firebase project, and so Google's terms for it, are OpenWorks IT's; the
  operator agreement should name Apple and Google as the services notifications go through.
- Review the audit log periodically (Settings > Audit report).
- Sharing a patient with a colleague is a disclosure inside the practice, for the patient's care. The consent statement should say that other psychologists at the practice may be given access for supervision or cover; the patient sees who in Settings.
