# What do you use Steady for between sessions?

Every psychologist will use Steady a little differently, and some of the most useful knowledge in this community will come from comparing notes. This topic is for how you use it in practice: what you send, when you look, what you bring into the room, and what you've stopped doing.

## What there is to work with

- **The daily check-in**: mood, anxiety, sleep and energy, each from 1 to 5, and a few words if the patient wants. On a patient's page you see the last fortnight as a line, with their words from each day.
- **Questionnaires**, sent once, daily or weekly, with an optional note such as "before Thursday's session". PHQ-9, GAD-7, PCL-5 and WHO-5 are built in, and you can write or import others (see Questionnaires and tools).
- **Exercises**: box breathing, 4-7-8 breathing, slow paced breathing, 5-4-3-2-1 grounding and a thought record, or exercises of your own. You can send them, and patients can also do them whenever they need them.
- **Daily words**: a sentence or two of your own that your patients see at the start of the day, before their check-in, alongside Steady's own (Settings > Practice > Library, then the quotation-mark button).
- **Watch data**, for patients who choose to share it (see "Talking to patients about watch data").
- **Around the edges**: short notes on a patient's page; a PDF report, an Excel workbook or an export for AI note-taking before a session; sharing a patient with a colleague (read only) for supervision or cover; and marking a patient as stable, which puts them at the bottom of your list with nothing highlighted.

## Some questions to start with

- How do you introduce Steady at the start of therapy? What do you say about who sees what, and how often you look?
- Do you send questionnaires on a schedule, or when something comes up in a session?
- What do you look at before a session, and do you go through it with the patient?
- Which exercises do patients actually do, and which do they ignore?
- Has anything become a burden, for you or for a patient? What did you change?

Made-up examples are welcome. If a real case would help, go through the de-identifying checklist, pinned in this category, before you post.
