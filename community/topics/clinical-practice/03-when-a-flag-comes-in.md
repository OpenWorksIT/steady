# When a flag comes in: how do you respond?

Steady highlights a few things for you to look at. What you do next is clinical judgement, and practices will do it differently. This topic is for sharing how you handle it: when you look, what you do with a PHQ-9 item 9 answer, how you cover leave. First, what Steady does and doesn't do.

## What raises a flag

- any answer above "not at all" on PHQ-9 item 9
- a questionnaire score in one of the higher bands (amber or red), and any review flag you set on a questionnaire of your own
- mood at 2 or below, out of 5, in each of the last three check-ins
- no check-in for three days or more (shown as information, not as a concern)

A questionnaire's flags stay on the patient's page for two weeks after it was completed. RADR has flags of its own, for watch data away from the person's usual, which you can mark as reviewed too; but RADR never alerts, and in your list it always comes after answers waiting for review. More in The science.

## How you hear about it

- A notification says only "New notification", for anything new, flag or not. You see what it is in the app, in Alerts and on the patient's page.
- Your patient list puts answers waiting for review first, the most serious first. Tap a patient's flags to see why they are where they are.
- Tap a flag to mark it as reviewed. It stays on the patient's record with your name and the date, and no longer moves them up your list.

## What Steady is not

- **Not an emergency service.** Nobody watches it around the clock, and it doesn't call or alert anyone on a patient's behalf. Every patient screen says so and shows the crisis numbers (SADAG 0800 567 567, and 112 from a mobile), and patients are told their psychologist will usually see what they send during working hours.
- **Not a message channel.** Patients can't message you through Steady. Whatever they write in a check-in reaches you when you open it.
- **Not always immediate.** If the practice's server can't be reached for a while, watch data waits on the phone and catches up when it's back, but a check-in or questionnaire answer can't be sent until then: the patient is told to try again. So something can reach you later than the patient meant it to.
- **Not a risk score or a diagnosis.** Flags are review aids.

## Questions for each other

- When in the day do you look, and do you look on days you don't see patients?
- What do you do with an item 9 answer that arrives at night or over a weekend? Steady's privacy notes leave each practice to decide whether it uses the PHQ-9 or the PHQ-8 (without item 9) between sessions, and how out-of-hours results are handled. What did your practice decide, and why?
- What do you tell patients at the start about what happens when a score is high?
- How do you record that you reviewed a flag: in Steady, in your own notes, or both?
- Who covers your patients when you're away, and do you share them with that colleague in Steady first?
