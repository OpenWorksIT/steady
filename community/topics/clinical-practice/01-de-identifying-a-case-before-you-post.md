# De-identifying a case before you post: a checklist
<!-- steady: pinned -->

The welcome topic sets the rule: nothing that could identify a patient, ever, in a topic or a personal message, in every category, the Tea room included. This page is the practical side of it: what to check before you ask about a real case. Keep it open while you write.

## Why the bar is higher here than in a journal

- **Readers are close by.** Members may work in the same city as you, some in the same practice. They may know the person, their family, their school, their employer or whoever referred them.
- **Your practice shows beside your name.** Readers already know where the patient is seen, which narrows things a great deal.
- **Small details add up.** An age, a job or a month is harmless alone. Two or three together can point to one person.
- **Posts last.** Editing keeps the earlier version, and the forum is backed up every day.

## Before you post

**1. Ask whether you need the case at all.** Many questions are really about a method, an instrument or a pattern. Ask it that way and leave the person out.

**2. Remove direct identifiers.**

- names, initials, nicknames and usernames
- dates of birth; ID, file, account and medical aid numbers
- phone numbers, email and street addresses
- the names of relatives, partners, employers, schools, churches, clubs, hospitals or other professionals involved

**3. Blur time.**

- no session, admission or incident dates: write "a few weeks into treatment", not "on 14 October"
- no exact ages: "an adult" or "a teenager", or a wide range if age matters to the question
- leave out the day of the week, the season and holidays unless they matter

**4. Remove place.** No towns, suburbs, farms, schools or workplaces. Say "rural" or "urban" only if it matters.

**5. Take out what's rare.** Rare conditions, unusual jobs, public roles, anything that was in the news, distinctive family facts (twins, a well-known relative), an unusual cause of injury or death. If the rare thing is the point, describe its kind ("an unexpected bereavement") instead of the thing itself.

**6. Change what doesn't matter.** You may change gender, age, occupation or family details that don't affect your question, and say that you have ("details changed"). Don't change what the question turns on.

**7. Check the combination.** Read your post three times: as the patient would, as their relative would, and as a colleague who might have seen them. Could any of them say "that's them"? If yes, or maybe, cut more.

**8. Third parties count too.** Family members, partners, colleagues and anyone who harmed the patient can also be identified.

## Steady's own data

- **No screenshots or photos** of any Steady screen with a patient on it: the patient list, a patient's page, check-ins, charts, RADR, results or exports. Not cropped and not blurred. (Steady's Feedback button blurs its own screenshots before they go to OpenWorks; a screenshot you take yourself is not blurred.)
- **Don't quote check-in words.** What patients write is theirs, and it can be recognised. Paraphrase, briefly.
- **Don't post a dated series** of scores or ratings. "The PHQ-9 rose from mild to moderately severe over a month" says enough.
- **Describe shapes, not data.** "Sleep later and more irregular for two weeks, then lower mood" carries the pattern without the person.
- **To show what a screen looks like**, describe it, or ask in Steady development for a screenshot of one of the fictional demo patients.

## A made-up example

> **Before:** A 34-year-old primary school teacher from a small coastal town, seen since March, whose twin brother died in a farm accident last year, scored 19 on the PHQ-9 on Tuesday and wrote "I can't face the classroom" in her check-in.
>
> **After:** An adult with a recent bereavement whose PHQ-9 rose into the moderately severe band over a few weeks, and whose check-ins mention dreading work. Details changed.

The question survives; the person doesn't.

## If it's still recognisable

Some cases can't be disguised without losing what matters. Take those to supervision, or to a colleague in person. A practice's private category, if it has one, is still part of this forum, and the same rule holds there.

## If something slips through

As the welcome topic says: don't edit it, because the earlier version is kept. Delete the post and tell an admin, who removes it for good. If it's someone else's post, flag it (the flag icon under it) and don't quote it.

What would you add to this list? If you have a way of writing about cases that works well, share it, without the case.
