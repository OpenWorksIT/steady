# Talking to patients about watch data

Sharing watch data is optional, and how it's introduced shapes whether a patient experiences it as support or as being watched. This topic is for how you talk about it: when you offer it, what you say, and how you use it in a session. How Steady works with the data (the measures, RADR and the research behind it) is in The science.

## What patients are told

When a patient connects a watch, Steady tells them, in plain words:

- **what is shared**: heart rate (the lowest, average and highest each day, and resting heart rate), sleep (how long, when, and its stages), steps, and blood oxygen if the watch measures it
- **how**: only daily totals and averages leave the phone, up to 30 days back when they connect and then new days as they use Steady
- **who sees it**: their psychologist, and a colleague if their psychologist shares their record (for example while away); it helps the psychologist understand how they are doing between sessions, and may be used, without their name, to improve care
- **what it doesn't do**: nobody watches it around the clock and it raises no alarms; it can help their psychologist decide whose information to look at first
- **their record**: it may be added to their clinical record, and once it is, the psychologist decides how it's used in their care
- **how to stop**: Settings > Stop sharing watch data; nothing new is added, and what was shared stays in the record

They agree to this separately from joining Steady. On their side, Steady shows only that watch data is shared and when it last synced; the charts are on yours.

## Points that come up

- **It isn't a mood reading.** A watch measures sleep, movement and heart rate, not how someone feels. It is context, read best beside what the patient says about the same days.
- **Usual is personal.** You see each measure against the patient's own usual range, not a population norm. A shift worker, a new parent or someone travelling will look "away from usual" for good reasons. You can stop counting a domain for one patient, with a reason (Patient settings > RADR measures).
- **Wear and sync matter.** Readings wait on the watch and phone until Steady syncs (when the app opens, or with Sync now), so nothing is lost if the patient doesn't open it for a while. For a useful resting heart rate, a Samsung watch should measure heart rate continuously (or every 10 minutes) and be worn to bed.
- **Permissions can lapse.** After a phone update, the phone sometimes stops letting Steady read watch data. The patient's page then says "Watch permissions seem to have lapsed", with a button to send them a reminder.

## Questions to start with

- When do you offer watch sharing: at the start, or later, once Steady is part of the work?
- How do you explain "nobody watches it around the clock" so that it reassures rather than sounds dismissive?
- Have you used watch data in a session? What did the patient make of it?
- Has anyone declined, or stopped sharing? What did you learn from why?

Made-up examples only, please; the de-identifying checklist is pinned in this category.
