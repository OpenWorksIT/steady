# Adolescents and Steady

Steady's questionnaires were chosen with adults in mind, but its consent statement covers younger patients too, and some practices will want to offer Steady to adolescents. This topic is for working out together how that goes well. It's a discussion, not guidance: bring what you've learnt and what worries you.

## Consent, as Steady has it

Steady's consent statement is the same for every practice. For patients under 18 it says:

> If you are 12 or older and understand what this means, you can agree to it yourself; your psychologist will talk it through with you first. If you are younger than 12, a parent or guardian agrees for you.

This follows the Children's Act 38 of 2005, which lets a child over the age of 12 consent to their own medical treatment if they are of sufficient maturity and have the mental capacity to understand its benefits, risks, social and other implications ([section 129](https://www.acts.co.za/childrens/129_consent_to_medical_treatment_and_surgical_operation.php)). Judging that maturity is yours, as it is for therapy itself.

## How Steady works for a younger patient

- **One account, the patient's own.** Only the patient signs in. There is no parent or guardian account, and Steady shows parents nothing.
- **One tick to agree.** When joining, the patient ticks once to agree to the consent statement. For a child under 12, how the parent's or guardian's agreement is given and recorded is for the practice to decide.
- **Deleting an account.** Any patient can ask to delete their account in the app (Settings > Delete my account). The practice does it within 30 days, after copying what belongs in the clinical record. A minor's records are kept until their 21st birthday ([HPCSA Booklet 9](https://www.hpcsa-blogs.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Booklet-9-Guidelines-on-Patient-Records.pdf), section 9.3.1).
- **Shared phones.** Notifications say only "New notification", so the lock screen gives nothing away. On a phone others use, Lock Steady (in the patient's Settings) asks for the phone's own screen lock when Steady opens.
- **Watch data.** RADR compares each person only with their own usual, so school holidays, exams or a new sleep pattern can move it. You can stop counting a domain for one patient, with a reason (Patient settings > RADR measures).
- **Questionnaires.** The built-in questionnaires were first validated with adults. Some have been studied in adolescents: for the PHQ-9, see [Richardson and colleagues (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2010-0852), in 13- to 17-year-olds. Check the evidence, and the licence, for anything you use with younger patients.

## Questions to start with

- How do you explain confidentiality, and its limits, to a 13-year-old and their parent in the same conversation?
- Do parents ask to see check-ins or scores? How do you handle it?
- Would you use Steady with a child under 12 at all? If so, who holds the phone?
- What do adolescents write in a check-in that they wouldn't say in the room, and how do you bring it back into a session?
- Which questionnaires have you found suitable for adolescents?

Made-up examples only, please; the de-identifying checklist is pinned in this category.
