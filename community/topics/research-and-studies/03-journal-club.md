# Journal club: how it works

A journal club here is simple: one paper a month, read by whoever can, discussed in replies over two weeks. No meeting, no slides. This is a proposal to start with; change it if something else suits people better.

## How it works

1. **Suggest a paper** as a reply here: a link (a DOI where there is one) and a line on why. Prefer papers everyone can read, open access or with a free copy.
2. **One paper is chosen** each month, and a topic is opened for it in Research and studies, titled "Journal club:" and the paper's short name.
3. **Someone opens the discussion** with a short summary: the question, the method, what was found and its limits.
4. **Everyone replies** over the next two weeks: what convinced you, what didn't, and what it means for between-session care in South African practice.
5. **Afterwards**, add the paper to The reading list, with a line on what the group made of it.

The usual rules hold: cite with a link, and if a paper brings a clinical example to mind, make it up.

## A first paper

Delgadillo J. and colleagues (2018). [Feedback-informed treatment versus usual psychological treatment for depression and anxiety: a multisite, open-label, cluster randomised controlled trial](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(18)30162-7). *The Lancet Psychiatry* 5(7):564–572. The authors' accepted version is free at [White Rose Research Online](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/129814).

Why this one: it tested feedback in ordinary care, with 2,233 patients of 77 therapists in eight NHS services in England, using the PHQ-9 and GAD-7, two of the questionnaires built into Steady. An algorithm alerted therapists to patients who weren't on track, to be reviewed in clinical supervision, and those patients ended treatment with less severe symptoms than in usual care (d = 0.23 on the PHQ-9, 0.19 on the GAD-7). The effects are small, the design open-label, and the service very different from private practice here: plenty to talk about.

Some questions to bring:

- Steady highlights answers and runs of low mood, but it doesn't compare a patient's progress with an expected course. Would that help, or get in the way?
- What did supervision add, and who would play that part in a small practice?
- How far do NHS services in England tell us anything about private practice in South Africa?

Who would like to open the first discussion? And is once a month right?
