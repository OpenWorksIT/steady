# The questionnaire library and licences
<!-- steady: pinned -->

Every questionnaire and exercise in Steady carries a licence status, and only cleared ones or the practice's own can be sent. This topic explains what's in the library, where each built-in instrument's licence stands, and how to check one you want to add. It's a summary for clinicians, not legal advice: the practice remains responsible for its right to use each instrument.

## Where to find it

Settings > Practice > **Library**. It has three sections: **Ready to send**, **Needs a licence check** and **Archived**. Each item shows its status (Cleared, Own, Check licence, Blocked or Archived). Tap one to see its licence, where it comes from and its questions.

## Waiting for permission

Kempie's measurement paper also recommends four instruments whose copyright holders publish no terms that cover Steady showing them in an app. They come into Steady once their holders agree:

| Instrument | Who to ask |
|---|---|
| DAST-10 | CAMH, which holds the copyright and licenses it ([CAMH DAST-10](https://research.mcmaster.ca/industry-investors/tech/camh-dast-10/)) |
| Penn Alcohol Craving Scale (PACS) | Its authors, Flannery, Volpicelli and Pettinati (1999); the Addiction Research Center that lists it can't grant permission ([ARC](https://arc.psych.wisc.edu/self-report/penn-alcohol-craving-scale-pacs/)) |
| Brief Situational Confidence Questionnaire (BSCQ) | Linda and Mark Sobell, through Nova Southeastern University's Guided Self-Change programme ([Guided Self-Change](https://www.nova.edu/guided-self-change/forms)) |
| 3-item Craving Scale | Roger Weiss and colleagues at McLean Hospital; NIH HEAL publishes it with no licence statement ([McHugh et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33086155/)) |

Until then, a practice that has its own permission can add any of them from a file, marked Check licence until a clinician confirms it.

## WHO-5 and the non-commercial clause

WHO-5 ships marked Check licence because its licence allows non-commercial use only, and a private practice charges for its services. Whether a practice's use with its own patients falls within that is for the practice to decide, with advice if it needs it. Once it has, a clinician confirms it in the library: tap WHO-5, then **Confirm the practice may use this**.

## Adding instruments

- **From the ReproSchema library** (Library > Add > Import): Steady lists a selection from this open research library, each with Steady's own note: "Listed as free to use", "Check the licence before use", or "Copyright protected: not available". Everything imported waits as Check licence until a clinician confirms the practice may use it.
- **From a file** (a Steady or FHIR questionnaire): also Check licence, unless you tick "This is the practice's own questionnaire". Tick it only for material the practice wrote.
- **Written in Steady**: marked Own and ready to send. See "Building your own questionnaire".

## How to check a licence

1. **Go to the source**: the developer's or publisher's own page, or the original paper, not a copy on another site.
2. **Find the terms of use.** The usual answers: public domain; free without permission; free with permission (often by email); free for non-commercial or research use only; or sold by a publisher.
3. **Check they cover what Steady does**: showing the items in an app, scoring them, and, if you adapt it, changing the wording or the scale.
4. **Check translations separately.** A translation may have its own licence, and its own evidence.
5. **Keep what you found**, the page or the email granting permission, and say so in a reply here, so nobody has to repeat the work.
