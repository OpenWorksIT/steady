# The questionnaire library and licences
<!-- steady: pinned -->

Every questionnaire and exercise in Steady carries a licence status, and only cleared ones or the practice's own can be sent. This topic explains what's in the library, where each built-in instrument's licence stands, and how to check one you want to add. It's a summary for clinicians, not legal advice: the practice remains responsible for its right to use each instrument.

## Where to find it

Settings > Practice > **Library**. It has three sections: **Ready to send**, **Needs a licence check** and **Archived**. Each item shows its status (Cleared, Own, Check licence, Blocked or Archived). Tap one to see its licence, where it comes from and its questions.

## What's built in

| PHQ-9 | Depression symptoms | Developed by Spitzer, Williams, Kroenke and colleagues with a grant from Pfizer. No permission is required to reproduce, translate, display or distribute it ([phqscreeners.com](https://www.phqscreeners.com/)) | Cleared |
| GAD-7 | Generalised anxiety | The same terms as the PHQ-9 | Cleared |
| PCL-5 | Post-traumatic stress symptoms | Developed by the US National Center for PTSD; in the public domain and not copyrighted ([National Center for PTSD](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/assessment/adult-sr/ptsd-checklist.asp)) | Cleared |
| WHO-5 | Wellbeing | © World Health Organization 2024, under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO: non-commercial use, with the source cited, adaptations under the same licence, and no WHO logo ([the WHO-5](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mental-health/who-5_english-original4da539d6ed4b49389e3afe47cda2326a.pdf)) | Check licence: each practice confirms |

The exercises (box breathing, 4-7-8 breathing, slow paced breathing, 5-4-3-2-1 grounding and the thought record) are OpenWorks IT's own wording of widely used techniques, under Steady's free software licence (AGPL-3.0).

## WHO-5 and the non-commercial clause

WHO-5 ships marked Check licence because its licence allows non-commercial use only, and a private practice charges for its services. Whether a practice's use with its own patients falls within that is for the practice to decide, with advice if it needs it. Once it has, a psychologist confirms it in the library: tap WHO-5, then **Confirm the practice may use this**.

## Adding instruments

- **From the ReproSchema library** (Library > Add > Import): Steady lists a selection from this open research library, each with Steady's own note: "Listed as free to use", "Check the licence before use", or "Copyright protected: not available". Everything imported waits as Check licence until a psychologist confirms the practice may use it.
- **From a file** (a Steady or FHIR questionnaire): also Check licence, unless you tick "This is the practice's own questionnaire". Tick it only for material the practice wrote.
- **Written in Steady**: marked Own and ready to send. See "Building your own questionnaire".

## How to check a licence

1. **Go to the source**: the developer's or publisher's own page, or the original paper, not a copy on another site.
2. **Find the terms of use.** The usual answers: public domain; free without permission; free with permission (often by email); free for non-commercial or research use only; or sold by a publisher.
3. **Check they cover what Steady does**: showing the items in an app, scoring them, and, if you adapt it, changing the wording or the scale.
4. **Check translations separately.** A translation may have its own licence, and its own evidence.
5. **Keep what you found**, the page or the email granting permission, and say so in a reply here, so nobody has to repeat the work.
