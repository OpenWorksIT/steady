# WHO-5: the wellbeing index

## What it is

The World Health Organization Five Well-Being Index asks how often, over the last two weeks, the patient has felt five positive states. It is Steady's built-in wellbeing measure (`who5`), described in the app as "Wellbeing, 5 items, about 1 minute". Unlike the other built-in questionnaires, **higher is better**.

## How it came about

The WHO-5 was one of the four instruments VR Clinical Psychology asked for from the start. It is very short, positively worded (a gentle questionnaire to send between sessions), and works both as an outcome measure and as a screen for depression.

Topp, Østergaard, Søndergaard and Bech (2015) reviewed 213 articles on it. They found it has adequate validity as a screening tool for depression and as an outcome measure, and recommended a cut-off of 50 or lower when screening for depression; across 18 screening studies, sensitivity was 0.86 and specificity 0.81. A stricter cut-off of 28 or lower has also been used, for example with a sensitivity of 0.93 and specificity of 0.83 in one study they included. The WHO's own 2024 edition of the index gives a percentage score below 50 as a suggested cut-off for poor mental wellbeing and a prompt for further assessment.

## How it works in Steady

**Instructions**: "Please indicate for each of the five statements which is closest to how you have been feeling over the last two weeks."

| Item | Text |
|---|---|
| 1 | I have felt cheerful and in good spirits |
| 2 | I have felt calm and relaxed |
| 3 | I have felt active and vigorous |
| 4 | I woke up feeling fresh and rested |
| 5 | My daily life has been filled with things that interest me |

**Answers** for every item: All of the time (5), Most of the time (4), More than half of the time (3), Less than half of the time (2), Some of the time (1), At no time (0). All five are required.

**Score**: the raw sum (0 to 25) times 4, giving 0 to 100. Because the raw score is a whole number, the total always moves in steps of 4: 0, 4, 8 … 48, 52 … 100. A total of exactly 50 can't occur.

| Total | Raw score | Band in Steady | Severity | A flag? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–28 | 0–7 | Very low wellbeing (≤28) | Alert (red) | Yes |
| 29–50 (in practice 32–48) | 8–12 | Low wellbeing (≤50) | Warn (amber) | Yes |
| 51–100 (in practice 52–100) | 13–25 | Above screening threshold | None | No |

So Steady's "50 or lower" band is the same as the WHO's "below 50 (a raw score below 13)". There are no item flags.

**Where it shows**: in your Alerts list ("[name] completed WHO-5", with the score and band), on the result screen with every answer, on the patient's flags for 14 days when 48 or lower, as a point on the RADR chart, and in exports. The patient never sees the score or band.

## Reading it beside the others

The WHO-5 runs the other way from the PHQ-9, GAD-7 and PCL-5: a falling WHO-5 is a worsening. On the RADR chart, questionnaire totals are plotted together, so take care when a WHO-5 line and a PHQ-9 line cross. Item 4 ("woke up feeling fresh and rested") overlaps with the check-in's sleep rating and, for patients who share watch data, with measured sleep.

## Limits

- **A wellbeing index, not a symptom measure.** A low score says wellbeing is low; it doesn't say why.
- **The two cut-offs serve different purposes.** 50 or lower is the recommended screening threshold; 28 or lower is a stricter one. "Very low wellbeing" is Steady's own label for the stricter band.
- **Two-week recall**, overlapping when sent weekly.
- **Coarse steps.** One answer moving one place changes the total by 4.
- **No change scores.** Steady shows totals over time but doesn't calculate change.
- **Studied in many countries and settings**, but the cut-offs were not set in South African private practice.

Views on the band labels, the red band at 28, and how the WHO-5 is best used between sessions are welcome in the replies.
