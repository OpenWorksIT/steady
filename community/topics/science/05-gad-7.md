# GAD-7: generalised anxiety

## What it is

The Generalized Anxiety Disorder 7-item scale asks how often, over the last two weeks, the patient has been bothered by seven core symptoms of generalised anxiety. It is Steady's built-in anxiety measure (`gad7`), described in the app as "Anxiety symptoms, 7 items, about 2 minutes".

## How it came about

The GAD-7 was one of the four instruments VR Clinical Psychology asked for from the start, as the anxiety counterpart to the PHQ-9: the same authors, the same answer format, the same two-week window and the same licence, so the two read naturally side by side.

Spitzer, Kroenke, Williams and Löwe (2006) developed and validated it in 2,740 adult primary care patients, 965 of whom had a telephone interview with a mental health professional. At a cut-off of 10, sensitivity for generalised anxiety disorder was 89% and specificity 82%. They proposed 5, 10 and 15 as the thresholds for mild, moderate and severe anxiety. Steady's bands are those thresholds.

## How it works in Steady

**Instructions**: "Over the last 2 weeks, how often have you been bothered by the following problems?"

**Answers** for every item: Not at all (0), Several days (1), More than half the days (2), Nearly every day (3). All seven items are required.

| Item | Text |
|---|---|
| 1 | Feeling nervous, anxious, or on edge |
| 2 | Not being able to stop or control worrying |
| 3 | Worrying too much about different things |
| 4 | Trouble relaxing |
| 5 | Being so restless that it is hard to sit still |
| 6 | Becoming easily annoyed or irritable |
| 7 | Feeling afraid as if something awful might happen |

Steady leaves out the unscored closing question about how difficult the problems have made daily life.

**Score**: the sum of the seven items, 0 to 21.

| Total | Band | Severity in Steady | A flag? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4 | Minimal | None | No |
| 5–9 | Mild | Info | No |
| 10–14 | Moderate | Warn (amber) | Yes |
| 15–21 | Severe | Alert (red) | Yes |

There are no item flags on the GAD-7.

**Where it shows**: in your Alerts list ("[name] completed GAD-7", with the score and band), on the result screen with every answer, on the patient's flags for 14 days when moderate or severe, as a point on the RADR chart, and in exports. The patient never sees the score or band.

**Licence**: Pfizer makes the PHQ screeners, the GAD-7 among them, free to use; no permission is required to reproduce, translate, display or distribute them. Steady marks it cleared.

## How to read it alongside the check-in

The daily check-in asks "How anxious or tense do you feel?" right now, on a 1 to 5 scale where higher is worse. The GAD-7 asks about two weeks of specific symptoms. The two can diverge for good reasons: a patient may feel calm at the moment they check in each evening and still report two weeks of uncontrollable worry. For patients who share watch data, the RADR chart shows GAD-7 totals beside the daily anxiety rating and the watch scores, so the two kinds of report can be compared over time. Neither is used to compute the other.

## Limits

- **Built for generalised anxiety.** Spitzer et al. (2006) validated it against generalised anxiety disorder; its use for other anxiety presentations rests on later studies not covered here. It is not a diagnostic interview.
- **Two-week recall.** Sent weekly, consecutive results overlap by a week.
- **Overlap with other states.** Restlessness, irritability and trouble relaxing also rise with depression, agitation, substance use, some medication and physical illness.
- **Severe starts at 15 by convention**, from a single primary care sample. The cut-points are reasonable starting points, not facts about any one patient.
- **No change scores.** Steady shows totals over time but doesn't calculate reliable or clinically significant change.
- **Validated elsewhere.** The original sample was North American primary care, not South African private practice.

Experience with the GAD-7 between sessions, and with how it sits beside the daily anxiety rating, is welcome in the replies.
