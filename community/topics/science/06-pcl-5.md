# PCL-5

## What it is

The PTSD Checklist for DSM-5 is a 20-item self-report of how much the patient has been bothered, over the past month, by each of the DSM-5 symptoms of posttraumatic stress disorder. It is Steady's built-in trauma measure (`pcl5`), described in the app as "PTSD symptoms, 20 items, about 6 minutes".

## How it came about

The PCL-5 was one of the four instruments VR Clinical Psychology asked for from the start; trauma is a core part of the practice's work. It maps item for item onto the four DSM-5 symptom clusters, it is in the public domain, and it is widely used to track change during treatment.

Blevins et al. (2015) described its development and first psychometric evaluation: in 278 trauma-exposed university students, internal consistency was 0.94 and test-retest reliability 0.82, with convergent correlations of 0.74 to 0.85, and a second sample of 558 gave similarly strong reliability and validity. Bovin et al. (2016) evaluated it in veterans and found scores of 31 to 33 optimally efficient for detecting probable PTSD. The US National Center for PTSD's current guidance says a cut-off between 31 and 33 is indicative of probable PTSD across samples, and suggests 10 points as an indicator of response to treatment.

## How it works in Steady

**Instructions**: "Below is a list of problems that people sometimes have in response to a very stressful experience. Please read each problem carefully and then choose how much you have been bothered by that problem in the past month."

**Answers** for every item: Not at all (0), A little bit (1), Moderately (2), Quite a bit (3), Extremely (4). All 20 items are required.

The 20 items follow the standard PCL-5 wording, in four clusters:

| Cluster (subscale in Steady) | Items | Subscale range |
|---|---|---|
| B: Intrusion | 1 to 5 (memories, dreams, flashbacks, upset at reminders, physical reactions) | 0–20 |
| C: Avoidance | 6 and 7 (avoiding memories and feelings; avoiding external reminders) | 0–8 |
| D: Negative cognitions and mood | 8 to 14 (amnesia, negative beliefs, blame, negative feelings, loss of interest, detachment, no positive feelings) | 0–28 |
| E: Arousal and reactivity | 15 to 20 (irritability, risk taking, hypervigilance, startle, concentration, sleep) | 0–24 |

**Score**: the sum of the 20 items, 0 to 80, with the four cluster subscales reported beside it.

| Total | Band | Severity in Steady | A flag? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–30 | Below provisional cut-point | None | No |
| 31–80 | At or above provisional cut-point (31–33) | Warn (amber) | Yes |

Every PCL-5 result carries this note: "No official severity bands. A change of 10 points is suggested by the National Center for PTSD as clinically meaningful."

There are no item flags. In particular, item 16, "Taking too many risks or doing things that could cause you harm?", raises no flag whatever the answer; you see it on the result screen with every other answer.

**Where it shows**: in your Alerts list ("[name] completed PCL-5", with the score and band), on the result screen with the subscales and every answer, on the patient's flags for 14 days at 31 or more, as a point on the RADR chart, and in exports. The patient never sees the score, band or subscales.

**Licence**: the PCL-5 was developed by the US National Center for PTSD and is in the public domain. Steady marks it cleared.

## What Steady doesn't do

- **No provisional diagnosis.** The PCL-5 can be scored against the DSM-5 rule (at least one B item, one C item, two D items and two E items rated 2, "Moderately", or higher). Steady doesn't apply that rule; the subscales are sums only.
- **No index event.** Steady's version is the 20 items alone. It doesn't ask which experience the patient has in mind, and the answers assume they have one.
- **No change score.** Steady shows totals over time but doesn't calculate change. The 10-point figure is in the note for you to apply.

## Limits

- **The cut-point comes from US samples**, mostly students and veterans. It is provisional by name, and South African clinical populations may differ.
- **One-month window.** Sent weekly, consecutive results overlap by three weeks; a weekly PCL-5 moves slowly by design.
- **Arousal and sleep items overlap** with depression, anxiety, pain and substance use.
- **The note's wording** says 10 points is "clinically meaningful"; the National Center for PTSD's own guide calls 10 points "an indicator of response". The difference matters when you report change.
- **A screening and monitoring measure, not a diagnosis.** Steady never treats a score as one.

Replies on the cut-point, the note, and whether item 16 should raise a flag are welcome.
